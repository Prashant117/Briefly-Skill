# Correlation, Causation and Confounders
*Lecture · 00:15:00 · Source: lecture-youtube.vtt*

## TL;DR
The lecture distinguished correlation from causation and explained confounders, randomisation and uncertainty. Students were assigned to draw a causal diagram for the ice cream example.

## Summary
The lecturer defined correlation as two measured variables varying together and stressed that association alone does not establish a causal effect. A confounder was introduced as something that influences both a proposed cause and its outcome, illustrated by temperature affecting both ice cream sales and swimming. Random assignment balances confounders in expectation, though small samples can still be imbalanced by chance. Confidence intervals communicate uncertainty, but a narrow interval cannot fix a biased measurement process. The lecture also recommended predefining outcomes, checking how participants were selected before generalising, and treating observational evidence as useful without proving causality.

## Key Points
- Correlation means two measured variables vary together; it does not by itself establish causation [00:00]
- A confounder influences both the proposed cause and the outcome, e.g. temperature with ice cream sales and swimming [02:00]
- Random assignment balances confounders in expectation [04:00]
- Small samples can still have accidental imbalance [05:00]
- A confidence interval communicates uncertainty, but a narrow one does not repair biased measurement [06:00]
- Predefining an outcome reduces selective reporting [08:00]
- Check how participants were selected before generalising results [09:00]
- Observational evidence can be useful without proving causality [10:00]
